PART 2  The project's own checksum: CRC-32
        600 runs. Mean 82,972 messages until the first collision (theory 82,138).
        Half of the runs had a collision within 79,043 messages (theory 77,163).
        A full search of all 2^32 = 4,294,967,296 values was never needed.
        Example: 91d6998a2fbb1e09b57781c2df4ecc95 and f9a36b3a38a87c3e2a3baf7552e8eedc both have CRC-32 5a7da048 (found after 38,688 messages).

PART 3  Using the attack on our own steganography (no password)
        Harmless message: 'Meeting moved to 5 pm, room 204. [ref HROMJN60]'
        Harmful message:  'Send me all the exam answers now [ref UZVEOP1D]'
        Both have CRC-32 4c5363bb. Found with 163,060 checksums in 0.08 s.

        Text carrier: hide the harmless message, then swap the hidden bytes and keep the checksum.
          reveal() before the swap: 'Meeting moved to 5 pm, room 204. [ref HROMJN60]'
          reveal() after the swap:  'Send me all the exam answers now [ref UZVEOP1D]'
          visible text unchanged:   True
          -> swap ACCEPTED, no error

        Image carrier: same idea on a PNG produced by embed().
          extract() before the swap: 'Meeting moved to 5 pm, room 204. [ref HROMJN60]'
          extract() after the swap:  'Send me all the exam answers now [ref UZVEOP1D]'
          -> swap ACCEPTED, no error

        With a password (AES-256-GCM, 128-bit tag that depends on the key):
          text carrier, one hidden character changed:  rejected
          image carrier, one embedded value changed:    rejected

PART 4  What a longer check value buys

Work for a 50% chance of a collision (this laptop: 5,635,862 checksums per second in Python)
  check_value_bits  used_for                                    tries_for_50_percent  time_on_this_laptop  time_at_one_billion_per_second
  ----------------  ------------------------------------------  --------------------  -------------------  ------------------------------
  32                CRC-32: both carriers without a password    77,163                under a second       under a second                
  64                a 64-bit check value                        5,056,937,541         15 minutes           5 seconds                     
  128               AES-GCM tag: both carriers with a password  2.17e+19              122,119 years        688 years                     
  256               SHA-256                                     4.01e+38              2.3e+24 years        1.3e+22 years                 

        Each extra bit of check value multiplies the work by 1.41; doubling the size squares it.
        The GCM tag also depends on the secret key, so an attacker cannot even compute it offline.
